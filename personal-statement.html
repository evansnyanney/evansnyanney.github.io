PERSONAL STATEMENT

From mathematics to measured geometry

My path through statistical theory, computational methods, and manufacturing research.

I grew up in Ghana and studied Mathematics and Actuarial Science at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology. I later earned my master's degree in Mathematical Sciences, with a focus on Applied Statistics and Data Science, at the University of Texas Rio Grande Valley. My master's thesis examined conditional optimal sets and quantization coefficients for uniform distributions, under the supervision of Prof. Mrinal Kanti Roychowdhury.

That mathematical foundation now informs my PhD research in Industrial and Systems Engineering at Ohio University, where I am advised by Dr. Zhaohui Geng. I develop statistical methods for 3D point-cloud registration and geometric uncertainty characterization in reverse engineering and additive manufacturing quality assessment. My first-author Manufacturing Letters paper investigates process and design relationships in point-cloud data using unsupervised learning.

My research brings these questions into applied settings. I collaborate on point-cloud methods for concrete printing and geometric quality assessment with Vitruvian Building. In healthcare, my work with collaborators at the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center addresses EEG artifacts and intraoperative cerebral ischemia detection. These applications connect my statistical training with the interpretation of manufacturing measurements and clinical signals.

My next research direction is to investigate how accelerated computing can make geometric analysis more efficient for large point clouds. I want to study the computational costs of registration and evaluate GPU approaches alongside geometric accuracy and statistical reliability. My aim is to develop methods that make 3D measurement more useful for engineering decisions.

Teaching and academic service are also part of my development. I have taught Elementary Statistical Methods as an instructor of record at UTRGV and tutored calculus and linear algebra. As a 2026–2027 IISE Future Faculty Fellow, I am continuing to prepare for a career combining research and education.
