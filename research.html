Research

RESEARCH

Using 3D measurements to inspect manufactured parts

I develop methods to align 3D scans, measure differences in shape, and understand uncertainty in those measurements.

What I study

A point cloud is a set of measured points that describes an object's shape. I study how to align point clouds and interpret differences between them. This work supports reverse engineering and the inspection of 3D-printed parts.

01

CURRENT RESEARCH

Aligning 3D scans and measuring uncertainty

I develop statistical methods to align 3D scans and assess uncertainty in geometric measurements. With Dr. Zhaohui Geng, I am preparing manuscripts on scan alignment, organizing point-cloud data, and statistical analysis after alignment.

I also collaborate with Vitruvian Building on using 3D scans to measure and assess the shape of printed concrete structures.

PUBLISHED WORK

Studying how manufacturing shapes a part

My 2025 first-author paper in Manufacturing Letters uses unsupervised learning to study how a part's design and manufacturing process relate to its measured 3D shape.

Read the paper ↗
03

FUTURE WORK

Making 3D analysis faster with GPUs

I plan to test whether GPUs can speed up the calculations used to align and analyze large point clouds. I will first identify the slowest steps, then compare CPU and GPU versions for speed, memory use, and alignment accuracy.

I also plan to test how scan size and measurement noise affect the results. This work is planned, and I have not yet measured a GPU speedup.

04

HEALTHCARE RESEARCH

Analyzing brain signals during surgery

With collaborators at the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center, I develop methods to detect interference in EEG recordings and signs of reduced blood flow to the brain during surgery. EEG records the brain's electrical activity.

My 2026 first-author paper uses a lightweight neural network to detect interference in continuous EEG recordings.
